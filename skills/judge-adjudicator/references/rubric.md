# Independent rubric, not a score-driven verdict

| Dimension | 0: evidenced blocker | 1: conditional/mixed | 2: sufficient for bounded next step | null |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| demand | Observed needs/behaviour conflict with the proposed next step | Limited/mixed signals; material assumptions remain | Relevant evidence is adequate for the claimed *limited* step, not future scale | Unassessed or no relevant evidence |
| feasibility | Concrete capability/time/resource constraint defeats the scope | Plausible with explicit prerequisites | The scoped implementation/test is credibly achievable within constraints | Unknown |
| viability | Known costs/incentives defeat the stated objective | Economics/incentives work only conditionally | Adequate for the stated goal (including a small community/learning goal) | Unknown or not yet meaningful |
| risk | Evidenced unacceptable safety/privacy/trust exposure for the scope | Material controls or permissions still needed | Risks acceptably bounded with explicit controls for that step | Not assessed; NOT 'safe' |
| goal_fit | The plan conflicts with the confirmed user goal | Partial alignment or a needed scope change | Directly advances the actual goal within constraints | Goal-dependent fit cannot be assessed |

- Do not add, average or weight scores into a verdict. Never display a success probability or 8/10 business score.
- Explain null as unknown or not applicable. Do not convert it to zero; the fixed five entries prevent silent omission.
- Evidence strength is cross-cutting, not a sixth numeric dimension. Behaviour > data > opinion > assertion is a fallible heuristic inherited from the evidence protocol, not a guarantee of truth or relevance.
- Small samples: preserve the current Paksh protocol's conservative strength cap at n<20 for compatibility. This is a configurable heuristic, not a statistical law. It does not erase an observed event; it limits generalisation. Demand for one manual session is not proof of demand for an app.
- Ratings and labels are proposed product policy, not a validated scientific instrument. Calibrate against human-reviewed cases and report misses before asserting reliability.
